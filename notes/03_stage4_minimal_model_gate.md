## T1: rejection theorem with source terminal selection
Independent costs across projects and firms, common support [c,C] with continuous strictly positive interior density phi; 0<k<C-c. At every first outcome, terminal actual effective-cost bidding or exit is selected as weakly dominant, as in source; firm 2 enters terminal a.s. Terminal continuation satisfactory at all histories and beliefs (not uniqueness).

Suppose firm 2 bids l2 in round 1 a.s. Conditional on firm 1 own second cost m, D1(m,n)=(n-m)_+-(n-m-k)_+. Given rival price x, difference from winning is x-l1-D1. Rival price independent of its n, so gain sign changes once at l1+h1(m), h1=E[D1]. Because phi>0 the first firm's best reply on interior is uniquely this threshold; truncate at C/exit when necessary. 0<=h1<=k.
For firm 2 type (l,n) with c+k<l<C and c<n<C, define D2(m,n)=(min(m+k,C)-n)_+-(m-n)_+. For 0<e<C-l, x in (l,l+e) yields interior rival l1=x-h1(m). Switching bid l to l+e loses first round exactly in switch band, changing profits by D2-(x-l). Exact gain:
Delta(e)=integral_l^(l+e) integral_c^C [D2(m,n)-(x-l)] phi(x-h1(m)) phi(m) dm dx.
D2>=0 everywhere and >0 for n<m<C. On a sufficiently small compact x interval the density argument is bounded away from support endpoints, so dominated convergence gives Delta'(0+) = integral D2 phi(l-h1)phi(m) >0. There is a profitable interior deviation for every such type (a positive measure set). Measurable deviation can be chosen from countable rational increments; no exceptional null type problem. Contradiction to BNE; no revised equilibrium required for disproof. Ties/exit unaffected for switched histories. This does NOT exclude all PBE with alternative terminal selections or all possible first-round strategies. No global uniqueness or existence claim.
Uniform [0,1]: h2(n)=k(1-n)-(k-n)_+^2/2; gain e*h2-e^2/2 for k<l<l+e<1. e=min(h2,1-l)/2. Exact rational test 3420, minimum positive 3/1280000. ## Q2 complete payoff map with supplementary reserve rules
Normalized independent U[0,1], k=.4. A: bids [0,1) and exit E; B: bids [0,1] and E. Equal admitted bids split equally; one admitted bidder paid 1; both E means no award. These conventions extend the original rules; do not attribute them to source. Terminal selected truthful effective-cost bid; terminal no-award at zero-probability boundaries defined by rule, no profit changes.
For own type (l,m), rival bid x, q=1 if b<x, 1/2 if b=x, 0 otherwise. Gain vs exiting: E[1{rival enters}q(x-l-D_i)+1{rival exits}(1-l+G_i^w-G_i^0)]. G1^w-G1^0=-D1; G2^w-G2^0=0. E action gain zero. If rival bids atomless locally, derivative f_j(b)[l-b+E(D_i|B_j=b,m)]. Necessary, not sufficient. For fixed m, payoff relative to exit is affine in l with slope minus win probability. Single-crossing gives weak monotonicity of optimal win probability in first cost; not sufficient to prove unique bid, pure equilibrium or monotonicity in m.
Boundary necessary condition in A: if firm 1 exits with strictly positive probability, firm 2 with l<1 has a profitable entry deviation from exit, since b↑1 yields limit gain (1-l)Pr(E1)>0 (bounded convergence, no admitted atom at 1). Thus simultaneous positive exit masses cannot occur in a continuous equilibrium of A. Does not prove existence or nonexistence.
